# Business Idea Scoring Rubric
*Reference guide for scoring ideas with ICE, RPS, and ALS frameworks. Last updated: 2026-03-13.*

---

## Why Three Frameworks

No single score captures what matters. Three lenses, weighted by priority:

| Framework | Weight | What It Measures |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| ICE | 40% | Is this a real opportunity worth testing? |
| RPS | 35% | Does it fit Steve's pipeline and generate revenue fast? |
| ALS | 25% | Can agents do most of the work without Steve's time? |

**Combined Priority Score = (ICE × 0.4) + (RPS × 0.35) + (ALS × 0.25)**

Score over 70/100: approve for testing.
Score 50-69: evaluate further, reduce dependencies.
Score under 50: kill or archive for later.

## ICE Scoring — Extended Definitions

ICE = Impact + Confidence + Ease. Each scored 1-10.

### Impact (1-10)
*If this idea works at scale, how much does it move the business?*

| Score | Definition | Construction/HOA/AI Example |
|-------|-----------|----------------------------|
| 1-2 | Marginal: <$5K annual revenue, no leverage | Blog post generates some traffic |
| 3-4 | Modest: $5K-$20K, Steve's time required for all delivery | One-off consulting gig, no repeatability |
| 5-6 | Meaningful: $20K-$100K, some repeatability | Jake pilot engagements ($8K-$15K × 10 customers = $80K-$150K/yr) |
| 7-8 | Significant: $100K-$500K, scalable with agents | Data Rehab SaaS: $300/mo × 500 construction companies = $1.8M ARR |
| 9-10 | Transformational: $500K+, agents do 80%+ of work | Full-stack CFO automation suite licensed to industry peers |

**Common mistake:** Scoring 9-10 too early. Score based on what you can realistically achieve in 12 months, not the theoretical ceiling.

### Confidence (1-10)
*How sure are we that this problem exists, buyers will pay, and Steve can win?*

| Score | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 1-2 | Pure hypothesis, no evidence | "I think construction companies might want X" |
| 3-4 | Anecdotal evidence only | "I've seen one forum thread complaining about this" |
| 5-6 | Multiple signals, one validation | "5 forum complaints + 1 person said 'yes I'd pay for that'" |
| 7-8 | Strong evidence, partial validation | "Steve has done this himself, 3 paying customers in pilot, 60% close rate on demos" |
| 9-10 | Proven market, validated offer | "Repeat customers, referrals, known price point, known buying trigger" |

**Common mistake:** Counting agent-generated research as validation. Only human "yes I would pay" counts.

### Ease (1-10)
*How hard is it to test this idea with a real customer in under 2 weeks?*

| Score | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 1-2 | Requires months of build, legal, or capital | Build a full SaaS product before first customer |
| 3-4 | Significant setup: 2-4 weeks, multiple dependencies | New agent type + landing page + SendGrid sequence |
| 5-6 | Moderate: 1-2 weeks, some dependencies | New outreach sequence + Calendly link + one review call |
| 7-8 | Easy: < 1 week, mostly reusing existing tools | Use Jake pipeline to send a new type of outreach to existing leads |
| 9-10 | Same-day test: reuse everything that exists | Send 5 emails to existing enriched leads with a new CTA |

**Common mistake:** Underestimating what "test" means. A test requires a real customer response — not internal tooling completion.

## RPS Scoring — Extended Definitions

RPS = Revenue ceiling + Pipeline fit + Speed to first dollar. Each scored 1-10.

### Revenue Ceiling (1-10)
*What is the realistic maximum annual revenue from this idea if it works?*

| Score | Revenue Ceiling |
|-------|----------------|
| 1-3 | < $50K/year |
| 4-5 | $50K-$200K/year |
| 6-7 | $200K-$1M/year |
| 8-9 | $1M-$5M/year |
| 10 | $5M+/year |

Note: Score what is achievable in 24 months without hiring a team. Not the theoretical TAM.

### Pipeline Fit (1-10)
*Can the existing Jake/HOA/agent infrastructure generate leads and close deals for this idea, without building new discovery channels from scratch?*

| Score | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 1-3 | Requires entirely new channel, new agents, new tooling | Selling to healthcare — nothing in current pipeline applies |
| 4-5 | Partially reusable — some agents apply, some new build required | HOA product targeting property managers — need new discovery source |
| 6-7 | Mostly reusable — existing pipeline with modified outreach | Data Rehab to Jake leads — same companies, new message |
| 8-9 | Fully reusable — same leads, same agents, new CTA | Upsell to existing Jake pilot customers |
| 10 | Already built — just need to turn on | A new feature for current Jake customers who are already buying |

### Speed to First Dollar (1-10)
*How many calendar days until the first dollar of revenue could realistically land?*

| Score | Timeframe |
|-------|-----------|
| 1-3 | > 90 days |
| 4-5 | 30-90 days |
| 6-7 | 14-30 days |
| 8-9 | 7-14 days |
| 10 | < 7 days (existing pipeline + existing offer + existing leads) |

## ALS Scoring — Extended Definitions

ALS = Agent automation % + Leverage ratio + Scalability ceiling. Each scored 1-10.

### Agent Automation % (1-10)
*What percentage of this idea's total work can agents perform without Steve's time?*

| Score | Automation % | What "80% by agents" actually means |
|-------|-------------|--------------------------------------|
| 1-3 | < 20% | Steve does almost everything (manual consulting) |
| 4-5 | 20-50% | Agents help with discovery and drafting; Steve closes and delivers |
| 6-7 | 50-70% | Agents do lead gen, enrichment, outreach, and draft deliverables; Steve reviews and approves |
| 8-9 | 70-90% | Agents find leads, enrich, send outreach, draft deliverables, follow up; Steve does final review + call |
| 10 | 90-100% | Steve's only involvement is banking the money |

**In practice for ClawOps:** 80% = discovery + enrichment + outreach + follow-up + dossier generation + draft deliverable, all agent-run. Steve approves the send and takes the sales call.

### Leverage Ratio (1-10)
*How many customers can Steve handle without proportionally increasing his time input?*

| Score | Leverage | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 1-3 | 1:1 (Steve time = customer count) | Custom consulting engagements, fully bespoke |
| 4-5 | 1:5 (Steve handles 5 customers per unit of time) | Templated service with agent-assisted delivery |
| 6-7 | 1:20 | Agent-run process, Steve does QA + calls |
| 8-9 | 1:100 | SaaS with onboarding; agents handle most customer interactions |
| 10 | 1:1000+ | Fully productized, automated SaaS |

### Scalability Ceiling (1-10)
*Does the business model hold at 10 customers? 100? 1000?*

| Score | Ceiling |
|-------|---------|
| 1-3 | Breaks at 10 customers (Steve's time is the constraint, can't hire out) |
| 4-5 | Works at 10-20 customers, then requires hiring |
| 6-7 | Works at 50-100 customers with additional agents + one hire |
| 8-9 | Works at 500+ customers; agents + platform carry the load |
| 10 | Infinite ceiling — pure SaaS, marginal cost near zero per customer |

## Combined Priority Score Formula

```
Priority = (ICE_total × 0.4) + (RPS_total × 0.35) + (ALS_total × 0.25)

Where:
  ICE_total = Impact + Confidence + Ease  (max 30, normalized to 0-10 by dividing by 3)
  RPS_total = Revenue + Pipeline + Speed   (max 30, normalized to 0-10 by dividing by 3)
  ALS_total = Automation + Leverage + Scale (max 30, normalized to 0-10 by dividing by 3)

Priority (0-100) = ((ICE/30) × 40) + ((RPS/30) × 35) + ((ALS/30) × 25)
```

**Decision thresholds:**
- **70-100:** Approve — build test, assign agent, run outreach
- **50-69:** Evaluate — identify what's pulling score down, address it, re-score
- **30-49:** Park — idea has merit but timing or ease is wrong; revisit in 60 days
- **0-29:** Kill — not worth tracking

## Common Scoring Mistakes

**Inflating Impact:**
Scoring Impact based on the theoretical TAM (total addressable market) rather than what Steve can realistically generate in 12 months without hiring. The construction ERP market is $2B — that doesn't make Jake a 10. Jake gets a 7 because Steve can realistically close 10-20 deals in year 1.

**Deflating Ease:**
Agents tend to score Ease low because they're thinking about building the full product. Ease should reflect the minimum viable test — a Calendly link and 5 emails, not a deployed SaaS application.

**Ignoring Kill Conditions:**
Every idea needs a kill date and kill condition. Without one, EVALUATING ideas sit in the list forever and pollute the priority queue. Default kill condition: "If we don't have 1 paying customer by [date 60 days from now], kill it."

**Double-counting Agent Capability:**
ALS Automation % should reflect current agent capabilities, not agents that could be built. If the capability doesn't exist today, score it lower and note the gap.
